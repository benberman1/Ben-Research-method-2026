# Obtaining and Organizing Coral Fragment Samples for Lateral Growth Analysis
### A Laboratory Protocol by Ben Berman

## Purpose
Isolating individual coral fragments and positioning onto transparent glass surfaces facilitates polyp growth in the lateral direction. Newly developed coral polyps are can be optimally imaged and analyzed by microscopy under high resolution due to relatively equal focal plane distribution, allowing proper visualization of polyp growth and morphology from pre-established colonies. 

## Equipment

- 4 sample coral colonies of different species to fragment and regrow as individuals. 
- 40 rectangular glass microscope slides (75 mm × 25 mm x 1 mm).
- 40 circular glass bottom plates (diameter of 35 mm).
- [Ista Instant Glue (4g)](https://www.tzong-yang.com.tw/en/goods.php?id=1365) for underwater coral-glass adhesion.
- Paper towel for glass surface and coral adhesion surface drying.

## Procedure
### <u>Sampling</u>
- Four coral species were selected from laboratory colonies (having either encrusting or branching growth structure). 
- Coral species included *Leptoseris spp.*, *Porites solida*, *Stylocoeniella spp.*(encrusting) and *Stylophora pistilata* (branching).
- Selection of species was based on previous knowledge of known rapid lateral growth.
- Once a desired experimental colony was identified, a section of the coral was fragmented for further fragmentation into individual samples.*

*Not to worry, corals are able to regrow and grow from fragmentation in ideal environmental conditions. It is actually a method of natural asexual reproduction! See here by [Lewis et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.251209).


### <u>Assembling</u>

- Glass was cleaned by washing with tap or sea water, dried by paper towel and further air dried.
- Coral aquarium fragmented samples were further fragmented to be used as individual sample replicates.
- Corals were broken in the water to obtain a desired individual fragment size of around 1 cm in length.
- The fragmented coral pieces were temporarily removed from the water for glass surface adhesion. Following water removal, the surface of the coral to contact the glass surface (bottom for encrusting, side for branching) was quickly dried using paper towel to minimize fragment time outside the water. 
- Adhesive glue was applied to a glass surface, and an individual coral fragment was pressed and glued onto the glass material, allowing coral fragment sample to stick on the glass surface for later lateral growth imaging.
- Coral fragments were positioned aside from the center of the clear glass material so newly laterally-grown coral polyps would be centered on the glass material for best microscopy examination and imaging.
- Glass surfaces with glued coral fragments were returned and submerged under sea water at 25°C to acclimate.
- A total of 20 individual fragment samples per coral species were plated, either on to glass slides (n=10) or glass bottom plates (n=10). See **Figure 1** for experimental visual.
- Allowed coral fragments to acclimate in experimental aquariums for lateral growth to occur.*

*Corals were placed in a maintained salt-water aquarium, following a similar set-up used by [Craggs et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3538).
