###Inroduction
Diverse species of algae were sampled from the rocky shallow reefs of the coast of Sdot Yam from light and dark habitats. These were then taken into the lab and photophysiology parameteres were measured by PAM (Pulse Amplitude Modulation). The goal of this excersize is to determine how photophysiology parameters differ between light and dark habitat algae.

###Methods
Algae samples from Sdot Yam froh dark habitats came from under shaded areas of rocky pools where no light was visible contacting them, while light habitat algae came from areas completely exposed to sunlight without any shading. These sampling were extracted by hand and sealed in plastic bags to transport bacl to the lab. These were then sorted to find optimally sized algae pieces to anaylse by PAM.

Parameters analyzed include Y(II) (Effective Quantum Yield of PSII), NPQ (Non-Photochemical Quenching), Fv/Fm (Photosystem II maximum potential quantum efficiency) and ETR (Electron Transport Rate). These parameters were all computed by the PAM, except for Fv/Fm which was calculated from PAM-derived parameters Fo (Minimum Fluorescence) and Fm (Maximum Fluorescence)  using the Fv/Fm equation ([Fm-Fo]/Fm). The paremeters Y(II), NPQ, Fv/Fm, and ETR were measured for each algae Taxon, with actual photsynthesis parameters Am (asymptotic maximum = Pmax= max photosynthesis), AQY (initial sloap = alpha = Fv/Fm), Rd (curve y intercept = dark respiration (normalized oxygen = positive values [usually negative]), and Ik (Am/AQY, light-limited to saturation-limited irradiance level) determined on average.

###Discussion
Overall, different algae taxons behaved differently in dark and light habitats, highlighting each species uniqueness in photoacclimation strategies. Y(II) and NPQ were not drastically different from light and dark habitats, but taxon values of this parameter shifted between habitats. This suggests that Photosystem II operating efficiency and heat-dissipated excess light energy shift between indivudal taxon upon in dark and light habitats, yet overall range of these parameters remains consistent in algae populations. In constrast, Photosystem II maximum efficiency was differently distributed with an overall shift to higher efficiency in dark habitats compared to light. This suggests algal photosystem II performs better in dark conditions to obtain more light energy. Electron transport rate was greater in taxon from light habitats compared to dark habitats, suggesting a greater rate of photosystem II electron transport when irrandices increases with more light energy available to perform this function. Apparent quantum yield and dark respiration rates were greater in dark habituated species, while mean asymptotic maximum photosynthetic rate and light saturation parameters were greater in light habituated species, indicating species in dark habitats had increased photosynthetic efficiency under low irradiance yet their metabolic demand was greater, while species in light habitats experienced strong adaptation to intense light environments, enhancing performance when irradiance was elevated.  

Future research and a follow-up experiment would be optimal to best see how these algae perform when they are transplatted from their original dark and light habitats to the opposite light conditions, and compare the photosynthetic aspects to those identical taxa that remained in their home habitat. This would allow not only better investigation of how these specific taxa exist in these ocean environemnts, yet would emphasize more strongly their photoaclimation abilities and provide strong comparisons regarding general algae and specific species trends when light challenges are present.
